# Sample 1

**Question:** On Myrtle Ave, what kinds of businesses that ran 10+ years closed since 2015, and what replaced them?

## Baseline: gemini-3.5-flash, no tools, no system prompt

The transformation of **Myrtle Avenue in Brooklyn** (specifically the stretch running through Fort Greene, Clinton Hill, and Bedford-Stuyvesant) since 2015 is a classic study in urban gentrification. 

During this period, many legacy businesses—some of which served the community for decades—closed due to rising rents, building redevelopments, owner retirements, or the economic strains of the COVID-19 pandemic. They were largely replaced by **mixed-use luxury developments, upscale dining, boutique retail, and corporate wellness/medical offices**.

Here is a breakdown of the kinds of legacy businesses (running 10+ years) that closed on Myrtle Avenue since 2015, along with specific examples of what replaced them.

---

### 1. Affordable Grocery Stores & Supermarkets
For decades, Myrtle Avenue relied on independent, budget-friendly supermarkets. Since 2015, several of these closed to make way for major residential developments, changing where and how locals buy food.
*   **The Legacy Business:** **Associated Supermarket** (490 Myrtle Ave)
    *   *Years in Business:* 40+ years.
    *   *Closed:* 2016.
    *   *What Replaced It:* The single-story supermarket was demolished to build a 7-story mixed-use luxury apartment building. The ground floor now houses a modern, upscale **Key Food (Dan’s Supermarket)**, representing a shift from a basic discount grocery to a more expensive, modernized supermarket model.

### 2. Soul Food, Diners, & Community Nightlife
Local diners, neighborhood bars, and soul food spots that acted as community living rooms for 10 to 20 years closed as the demographic of the neighborhood shifted.
*   **The Legacy Business:** **Five Spot Soul Food** (459 Myrtle Ave)
    *   *Years in Business:* ~15 years.
    *   *Closed:* 2015.
    *   *What Replaced It:* A popular hub for live music, comedy, and soul food, the building was sold and redeveloped. The site was turned into a mixed-use building with luxury apartments upstairs and modern retail on the ground floor. 
*   **The Legacy Business:** **Maggie Brown** (332 Myrtle Ave)
    *   *Years in Business:* 15 years (opened in 2003).
    *   *Closed:* 2018.
    *   *What Replaced It:* **Evelina**, a stylish, high-end Mediterranean restaurant serving artisanal cocktails and upscale pasta—catering directly to the influx of wealthier residents in Clinton Hill.

### 3. Neighborhood Service & Hardware Stores
Mom-and-pop service businesses that catered to long-time brownstone owners and renters have increasingly been priced out.
*   **The Legacy Business:** **Clinton Hill Hardware** (452 Myrtle Ave)
    *   *Years in Business:* 20+ years.
    *   *Closed:* Around 2015/2016.
    *   *What Replaced It:* The old-school hardware store was replaced by a rotating cast of boutique retail and dining concepts. This trend was mirrored across the avenue as shoe repairs, laundromats, and local car services closed.
*   **The Legacy Business:** **Myrtle Car Service** and similar local dispatch offices
    *   *Closed:* Mid-to-late 2010s (disrupted by rideshare apps like Uber/Lyft and rising storefront rents).
    *   *What Replaced Them:* Specialty coffee shops, boutique fitness studios, and trendy quick-service food spots.

### 4. Eco-Friendly & Specialty Local Retail
Even early-wave gentrification businesses (shops that opened in the late 2000s and helped define the "new" Myrtle Ave) hit their 10-year marks and closed during the pandemic era.
*   **The Legacy Business:** **Green in Brooklyn** (432 Myrtle Ave)
    *   *Years in Business:* 11 years (opened in 2009).
    *   *Closed:* 2020.
    *   *What Replaced It:* **Scatola**, a chic, specialty Italian cafe, sandwich shop, and imported dry goods store.

### 5. Historic Fast-Food Anchors
While not "mom-and-pop" shops, certain corporate fast-food joints served as community landmarks and late-night gathering spots for decades.
*   **The Legacy Business:** **White Castle** (532 Myrtle Ave)
    *   *Years in Business:* Decades.
    *   *Closed:* Late 2014 / early 2015.
    *   *What Replaced It:* A massive, multi-story luxury residential building called "Myrtle Court." The ground floor retail space was carved up to house a **CityMD Urgent Care** and other modern, corporate retail storefronts.

---

### Summary of the "Replacement" Trend:
If you look at the macro-trend on Myrtle Avenue since 2015, the commercial corridor has experienced a distinct shift:

| Old Business Type (Closed) | New Business Type (Replacement) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Low-margin retail** (Hardware, 99-cent stores, laundromats) | **High-margin specialty retail** (Plant shops, boutique pet stores, luxury salons) |
| **Affordable food** (Diners, bodegas, Chinese takeout, fast food) | **Trendy/Experiential dining** (Artisanal bakeries, natural wine bars, New American bistros) |
| **Single-story commercial properties** | **Multi-story mixed-use developments** (Luxury rentals on top, national chains like Starbucks, Chase Bank, or urgent cares on bottom) |

Since 2015, 29 long-running businesses operating for 10 or more years closed on Myrtle Avenue, including several bodegas, pharmacies, electronics stores, and bars [E3]. While many of these properties lack recorded replacement occupants, others have transitioned into new retail shops, organic markets, restaurants, and national brands like Skechers [E3, E10, E12]. Successor businesses range from direct industry replacements to entirely new service uses [E3].

A wide variety of long-running storefronts closed along Myrtle Avenue after more than a decade of operation:

*   **Bodegas, Delis, and Grocers:** Goldstar Chen Inc at 68-03 Myrtle Ave closed in 2022 after 22.3 years [E18]. In 2024, Myrtle Avenue Shop, Inc. at 54-02 Myrtle Ave closed after 21.2 years [E18], Myrtle Convenience Store Inc at 55-17 Myrtle Ave closed after 18.3 years [E18], and Spring Florists Inc. at 56-11 Myrtle Ave closed after 11.8 years [E18]. In 2025, Deli-licious Delicatessen Inc at 71-02 Myrtle Ave closed after 16 years [E9], and Myrtle Market Place Corp at 1218 Myrtle Ave closed after 12 years [E9]. *   **Bars and Restaurants:** Caribe Billards, Inc. at 54-19 Myrtle Ave closed in 2021 after 27.7 years [E18]. Vaqueros at 1541 Myrtle Ave closed in 2021 after 12.3 years [E3]. Kitchen & Window Distributing Outlet Inc. at 68-01 Myrtle Ave closed in 2023 after 20.1 years [E18]. 
*   **Other Services:** Bedford Auto Sales at 984 Myrtle Ave closed in 2025 after 22.1 years [E9], and New York International Builders Corp. at 79-01 Myrtle Ave closed in 2025 after 11.3 years [E9].

## What the data can't tell us

*   **Reasons for Closures:** The local news coverage of individual neighborhood storefront closures along Myrtle Avenue is extremely sparse or nonexistent [E13]. Consequently, researchers could not find specific public reporting or reasons for the vast majority of these closures [E13]. 
*   **Unidentified Replacements:** For most of the 29 long-running closures on Myrtle Avenue, including major spaces like ARD Pharmacy and Stop & Shop, researchers found no successor business licenses or storefront registry records to identify replacement tenants [E3].
*   **Conflicting Database and Press Records:** License records and public media sometimes show contradictions. For example, local license data lists Care Express Pharmacy as closed in 2025 [E9], but active insurance and pharmacy registries still show it operating as an active independent community retail pharmacy [E13]. Similarly, license records indicate Stop & Shop #508 closed in 2025 [E9], but Patch reported in late 2022 that the store underwent a major remodeling investment and was excluded from corporate 2024 closure lists [E11].
